Waste Oils — Uzbekistan

Preliminary environmental assessment of emissions and damage from burning waste oils in boilers and greenhouses.

Prepared byEcoAudit-Central Asia
Date14 November 2026
StatusPreliminary
For Internal Official Use Only

1. Basis and Objective

This report has been prepared on the basis of:

  • open data from raids by the Special Environmental Commission (December 2025);
  • estimates of waste oil generation in Uzbekistan (~250,000 t/year);
  • international reference emission factors (EPA, IPCC, Japanese methodology for waste oil);
  • international damage cost estimates (market CO₂ price, SCC, EPA rates for PM/NOx/SO₂).

Purpose: to provide an order-of-magnitude damage estimate where official calculations are absent, and to propose short-term solutions.

4.2. Discharge of 75,000 t to Soil/Water

There are no direct international "tariffs." The lower bound based on soil treatment cost (~$1/t) is ~$75,000, but this does not account for groundwater migration or health impacts.

4.3. Unaccounted 50,000 t

Not assessed. Conservatively, this could be attributed to burning or discharge, increasing the total by 20–40%.

6. Short-Term Solutions

6.1. Conversion to Biodiesel with Pre-Treatment

Waste oil, after pre-treatment (filtration, removal of water and mechanical impurities, neutralization), can be processed into biodiesel.

6.3. Environmental Effect

Processing ~25,000 t/year of waste oil instead of burning/discharging reduces PM/PM-10, NOx, SO₂, and CO₂ emissions, prevents oil from entering soil and water, and reduces pressure on groundwater.

Note: 25,000 t is about 10% of total oil generation (250,000 t) and about 20% of the burning stream (125,000 t).

7. Brief Calculation for All of Uzbekistan

The calculation fully coincides with Section 3, since 125,000 t is already the national estimate of the burning stream.

8. Recommendations for Companies (Short-Term, 0–12 Months)

  1. Greenhouse farms and boiler houses: install particulate/gas filters; stop burning waste oil, tires, bitumen, and fuel oil; conclude contracts for waste oil removal.
  2. Collectors and processors: organize oil collection points; implement pre-treatment; launch pilot mini-plants.
  3. Service and transport companies: hand over waste oil only under contract; maintain records.
  4. Investors and mini-plant operators: replicate modular plants according to the scheme; ensure pre-treatment.

9. Limitations and Caveats

  1. This calculation is not official and cannot be used for legal claims.
  2. Emission factors are taken from international reference books, not from measurements.
  3. The 50/30/20 balance hypothesis is an estimate.
  4. Damage from discharge to groundwater is not calculated due to lack of monitoring data.

10. Next Steps (Internal)

  1. Request actual collection volumes from Uz-Prista Recycling for 2020–2025.
  2. Initiate field measurements of emissions from greenhouse boilers.
  3. Conduct soil and groundwater sampling in areas of mass burning.
  4. Launch a pilot mini-plant (1 t/day) and measure actual biodiesel yield.
