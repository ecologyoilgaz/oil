Waste Oils — Tajikistan

Assessment of Generation and Damage. Preliminary Environmental Report.

Prepared byEcoAudit-Central Asia
Date15 November 2026
StatusPreliminary
For Internal Official Use Only

1. Generation Volume

According to a study by the Technological University of Tajikistan (2022), the country annually imports more than 80,000 tonnes of technical oils and lubricants. Of this, more than 55,000 tonnes is generated as waste petroleum products entering the environment.

This is a confirmed scientific estimate with reference to data from the Agency on Statistics under the President of the Republic of Tajikistan — a more reliable basis than the Uzbekistan hypothesis, but the flow structure (burning/dumping/collection) is unknown.

2. Infrastructure and Official Position

The President of Tajikistan, Emomali Rahmon, acknowledged in September 2025 that the country lacks waste processing enterprises, including for waste oils. Existing small enterprises do not cover generation volumes.

Scientific research focuses on using local bentonite clays for oil regeneration — an attempt to find a cheap local solution instead of imported technologies.

3. Damage Assessment (Assumption)

Since the flow structure is unknown, we adopt the same hypothesis as for Uzbekistan (50% burning / 30% dumping / 20% unaccounted collection) as an assumption:

  • Burning: 27,500 t
  • Dumping: 16,500 t
  • Unaccounted collection: 11,000 t

Damage calculation for burning 27,500 t using the same coefficients:

ComponentLower estimateUpper estimate
CO₂ (27,500 × 2.9 × $21–1013)~$1.7 mln~$81 mln
PM/PM-10~$31 mln~$175 mln
NOx~$0.3 mln~$1.2 mln
Total burning~$33 mln~$257 mln

Dumping of 16,500 t: without monitoring data — cannot be determined.

4. Short-Term Solutions

  1. Create a collection system — receiving points in Dushanbe, Khujand, Bokhtar.
  2. Use local sorbents — bentonite clays for pre-treatment before processing.
  3. Pilot biodiesel processing — modular unit (1 t/day) with pre-treatment.
  4. Monitoring — soil and groundwater sampling in areas of high oil generation.

5. Limitations

  1. The calculation is not official and cannot be used for legal claims.
  2. The flow structure (50/30/20) is an assumption, not confirmed statistics.
  3. Specific emissions are taken from international reference books, not from measurements.
  4. Damage from dumping into groundwater has not been calculated due to lack of monitoring data.
