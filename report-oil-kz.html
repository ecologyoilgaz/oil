Waste Oils — Kazakhstan

Assessment of Generation, Collection, and Damage. Preliminary Environmental Report.

Prepared byEcoAudit-Central Asia
Date15 November 2026
StatusPreliminary
For Internal Official Use Only

1. Generation Volume

The total flow of waste oils in Kazakhstan is estimated at 120,000–180,000 tonnes per year (EEESEF analytical report, 2026). Of this:

  • 60,000–80,000 t — from automotive and commercial transport;
  • 60,000–90,000 t — from industry and mining (Tengiz, Kashagan, Karachaganak, Karaganda coal basin, Balkhash copper smelter).
Caveat: This is an analytical estimate based on lubricant consumption (~200,000 t/year), not official statistics.

2. Collection Rate and Infrastructure Gap

The official collection rate for waste oils in Kazakhstan is estimated at only 20–30% — the bottom quartile of global practice (Germany and France — 78–82%, USA — about 60%, China — about 80%).

20–30%Official Collection
70–80%Unaccounted
84k–144k tBurned or Dumped

Key figure: If only 20–30% is collected, then 70–80% of oil (≈ 84,000–144,000 t/year) goes to burning, dumping or unaccounted circulation.

3. Burning and Illegal Processing

Unlike Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan has documented criminal cases:

  • Aktobe, 2026: the head of IE "Kazenergy" was sentenced to 3 years 9 months for illegal processing of hazardous waste. Waste oils and oil sludge mixed with naphtha were used as feedstock. More than 11 million litres of furnace fuel were produced and sold, with criminal proceeds of 778 million tenge. The investigation noted that the fuel did not meet standards and could release toxic substances when burned in boilers.
Confirmation: The flow "waste oil → illegal fuel → burning in boilers" is real and has criminal evidence.

4. Damage Assessment (Calculated)

If we assume that 50% of 120,000–180,000 t/year is burned (60,000–90,000 t), then using international coefficients:

ComponentLower estimateUpper estimate
CO₂ (60–90k t × 2.9 t/t × $21–1013/t)~$3.7–5.5 mln~$176–264 mln
PM/PM-10 (per EPA)~$68–102 mln~$381–571 mln
NOx~$0.7–1.1 mln~$2.5–3.8 mln
Total burning~$72–109 mln~$560–839 mln

Dumping of 70–80% of uncollected oil (84,000–144,000 t): without groundwater monitoring data, cannot be determined.

5. Short-Term Solutions

  1. Increase collection — create a network of receiving points in industrial regions (Aktobe, Karaganda, Atyrau).
  2. Legalise processing — simplify licensing for small processors while maintaining environmental standards.
  3. Monitoring — field measurements of emissions from boilers running on illegal fuel.
  4. Pilot units — modular processors (1 t/day) with pre-treatment in 3–4 regions.

6. Limitations

  1. The calculation is not official and cannot be used for legal claims.
  2. Specific emissions are taken from international reference books, not from measurements at Kazakh boilers.
  3. The "50% burning" assumption is a hypothesis, not confirmed statistics.
  4. Damage from dumping into groundwater has not been calculated due to lack of monitoring data.
