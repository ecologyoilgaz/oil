Waste Oils — Kazakhstan

Generation, Pollution Load, Damage Assessment and Short-Term Solutions.

Prepared byEcoAudit-Central Asia
Date15 November 2026
StatusPreliminary
For Internal Official Use Only

1. Basis and Objective

This report has been prepared on the basis of open analytical data on waste oil generation in Kazakhstan (EEESEF, 2026), documented criminal cases of illegal processing and burning, international reference emission coefficients, and international damage valuations.

Objective: to provide an order of magnitude of pollution load and damage where official calculations are absent, and to propose short-term solutions.

2. Generation Volume

The total flow of waste oils in Kazakhstan is estimated at 120,000–170,000 tonnes per year (EEESEF analytical report, 2026). Of this:

  • 60,000–80,000 t — from automotive and commercial transport;
  • 60,000–90,000 t — from industry and mining (Tengiz, Kashagan, Karachaganak, Karaganda coal basin, Balkhash copper smelter).
Caveat: This is an analytical estimate based on lubricant consumption, not official statistics.

5. Soil Load

Dumping of 84,000–136,000 t/year of oil. At an average density of 0.9 t/m³, this is ~93,000–151,000 m³ of liquid hydrocarbons entering soil and ground. One litre of waste oil can contaminate thousands of cubic metres of water — the potential volume of water contamination is orders of magnitude larger than the dump volume itself.

7. Documented Cases of Illegal Circulation

Aktobe, 2026: The head of IE "Kazenergy" was sentenced to 3 years 9 months for illegal processing of hazardous waste. Waste oils, oil sludge and other oil-containing waste mixed with naphtha were used. More than 11 million litres of furnace fuel were produced and sold, with criminal proceeds of 778 million tenge. The investigation noted that the fuel did not meet standards and could release toxic substances when burned in boilers and furnaces.

8. Infrastructure Gap

Kazakhstan does not have specialised regeneration capacities (Group I or Group II). This means that even collected oil cannot be processed into base oil within the country. The collection rate is estimated at 20–30% — the bottom quartile of global practice (Germany and France — 78–82%, China — about 80%).

9. Short-Term Solutions

9.1. Biodiesel production with pre-treatment

Waste oil, after pre-treatment (filtration, removal of water and mechanical impurities, neutralisation), can be processed into biodiesel.

9.3. Environmental effect

Processing ~35,000 t/year of waste oil instead of burning/dumping reduces PM/PM-10, NOx, SO₂ and CO₂ emissions from burning; prevents ~35,000 t of oil from entering soil and water; reduces the load on groundwater in areas of mass burning (Aktobe, Karaganda, Atyrau).

Note: 35,000 t is about 20–30% of total generation (120,000–170,000 t) and about 25–40% of the burning flow (60,000–85,000 t).

10. Recommendations to Companies (Short-Term, 0–12 Months)

  1. Boiler houses and industrial facilities: install dust and gas cleaning filters on existing boilers; stop burning waste oil, tyres, bitumen, fuel oil; conclude contracts for waste oil removal with licensed collectors.
  2. Collectors and processors: organise oil receiving points in each industrial region; implement pre-treatment of oil before biodiesel processing; launch pilot mini-plants (1 t/day) in 2–3 regions to test the scheme.
  3. Service and transport companies: hand over waste oil only under contract, with transfer acts; keep records of oil generation and transfer.
  4. Investors and mini-plant operators: replicate modular units according to the scheme (regions × 6 units); ensure pre-treatment of feedstock for stable biodiesel yield.

11. Limitations

  1. The calculation is not official and cannot be used for legal claims.
  2. Specific emissions are taken from international reference books, not from measurements at Kazakh boilers.
  3. The "50% burning" assumption is a hypothesis, not confirmed statistics.
  4. Damage from dumping into groundwater has not been calculated due to lack of monitoring data.
  5. The scheme of 102 units × 1 t/day is illustrative; actual capacity depends on collection logistics and feedstock quality.

12. Next Steps (Internal)

  1. Request from the authorised bodies actual collection volumes for 2020–2025 to verify the balance.
  2. Initiate field measurements of emissions from boilers (PM, NOx, SO₂, Pb).
  3. Conduct soil and groundwater sampling in areas of mass burning.
  4. Launch a pilot mini-plant (1 t/day) and measure actual biodiesel yield after pre-treatment.
  5. After obtaining data — recalculate the report according to national methodologies.
